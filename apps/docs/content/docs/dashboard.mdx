Sign in with `ADMIN_EMAIL` and `ADMIN_PASSWORD` from your `.env`. Anyone who can reach the dashboard can also create an account at `/sign-up`, so keep a public instance behind your own access control. Passwords are at least 10 characters. "Forgot password?" shows only when the server can send email, which is not set up in this version, so a lost password cannot be recovered from the dashboard yet. Sign in, and Continue with GitHub when `GITHUB_CLIENT_ID` is set, are limited per address by the api. That is per client only when `TRUSTED_PROXY_COUNT` matches your proxies: with `0` every user shares one bucket per endpoint, so one person can lock sign in for everyone, and with `1` the dashboard must sit behind a proxy that overwrites `X-Forwarded-For`, see [deploy](/docs/deploy). The sidebar has these pages.

## Answers

The answer bank holds what skiffr fills for a question. Add, edit and delete answers, and see how often each was used and corrected. The suggestions panel proposes which learned answers to keep or drop, see [how skiffr learns](/docs/learning). Nothing is changed without you resolving it.

`GET /v1/answers/provenance` (`answers.provenance`, session only, the extension token can't call it) returns, for the same page of the bank as the answer list, where each answer came from: learned from a correction, confirmed, typed in, imported or drafted by AI. That is its origin, not its status, so a learned answer you rejected still says learned. It adds the sites and number of forms the question was recorded on and the first site it was seen on, counting every recorded field with that question whatever filled it (your profile, the bank, a model or a saved site). `lastUsedAt` is only the last fill from the answer bank. `conflict` says you corrected such a field to something other than the answer's current value: how many different values and on which sites, never the values, and an edit to the answer makes older corrections count as conflicts. All of it comes from the fields recorded after fills, which are kept for 90 days, so an answer with no recent fills shows none. A form is one batch of recorded fields.

## Queue

Paste a job link, with an optional title and company, or let [Gmail](/docs/gmail) and [Sources](/docs/sources) fill it. Filter by status (queued, applied, skipped) and source, search by company or role, and use **Open next** to go through the queue in order. Mark a job applied or skipped, or restore it to the queue. The same posting is queued once, whatever way it arrived. You always press submit yourself.

## Stats

Applications per day and week, the queue funnel, replies by the furthest stage reached, breakdowns by source, ATS, match score and role, time to apply, and an autofill section: fields skiffr filled and whether you left, corrected or cleared them. Pick a range at the top. Percentages only show from 5 applications or filled fields, before that they would be noise. The autofill section counts the fields recorded after a skiffr fill, so it fills up as you use the extension. Fields skiffr did not touch are not counted.

## Activity feed

`GET /v1/activity` (`activity.list`, session only, the extension token can't call it) lists what happened in your account, newest first, in pages: jobs queued per source and hour, applications and the stage they reached, answers learned or corrected, fields filled per site and hour, and failed Gmail syncs and source runs, and tokens that expired. Pass `since` to see only what happened at or after it, and `cursor` from the last page to get the next one. Keep `since` the same while paging: a cursor is not tied to it. Jobs queued and fields filled are counted per UTC hour and shown at the start of that hour, or at `since` when `since` falls inside the hour (only the rows from `since` on are counted then).

It is built from rows skiffr already keeps, so there is nothing extra stored and nothing to purge. It shows counts and short labels: never an answer or field value, a job link, an email address or a token. Text from pages and emails has hidden and direction-changing characters shown as code points and is cut to 120 characters. It looks back 30 days at most. A successful Gmail sync or source run is not an event of its own: the jobs it queued are.
